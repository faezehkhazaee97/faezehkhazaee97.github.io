About

Click the photo

Faezeh (Baran) Khazaee is an M.Sc. student in Computer Science at the University of California, Davis (expected December 2026), where she works with Gerald Quon on machine learning for single-cell and genomic data.

Her core strength is deep learning: transformers, self-supervised and representation learning, and generative modeling. She cares about careful benchmarks and interpretable models, so a prediction about a patient or a gene can be traced to the cells and sequences that drove it.

Research

My projects share one question: what can a deep model learn about biology from raw data, and can we see how it learned it?

1 / 4
Single cells

CELLECTION

Treats each sample as a set of cells and learns which combinations of cell states predict a sample-level phenotype: disease status, COVID-19 severity, developmental stage, even wing shape across birds from their gene content. I led benchmarking, validation and figures.

Benchmark code

Gene regulation

P2G

Predicts a gene’s expression as the sum of its promoters’ contributions, read from DNA plus measured histone marks or accessibility. A controlled benchmark against Enformer shows chromatin and sequence carry complementary information.

Language models for DNA

ProMod

A promoter language model pretrained on 162 genomes and fine-tuned on 35 species. It learns an organ code that transfers to species it never saw, and shows how much cross-species accuracy really comes from gene families.

Inside transformers

Boundary markers

What does a “|” every few characters do to an LLM? Across seven tasks and nine models: it re-tokenizes, helps on long sequences, and leaves a blind spot right after each marker.

Code

Tokenization

GenomeRL

DNA has no spaces. A reinforcement-learning agent learns where DNA tokens should begin and end, for promoter detection.

Varformer and EpiCRISPR

Varformer: a set-based transformer for rare-variant disease risk in brain disorders. EpiCRISPR: what decides the outcome of CRISPR epigenome editing.
