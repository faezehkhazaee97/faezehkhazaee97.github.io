About

Faezeh (Baran) Khazaee is an M.Sc. student in Computer Science at the University of California, Davis (expected December 2026), where she works in the Quon Lab on machine learning and computational biology.

Her core strength is deep learning: transformers, self-supervised and representation learning, and generative modeling. She applies these to large-scale single-cell and genomic data, and earlier applied them to computer vision and multimodal reasoning. She cares about models that are interpretable, so a prediction about a patient or a gene can be traced to the cells and sequences that drove it.

Before UC Davis, she studied information technology engineering at the University of Tehran and Sharif University of Technology, and built machine learning systems as a freelance data scientist.

Research

My projects share one question: what can a deep model learn about biology from raw data, and can we see how it learned it? Most pair a transformer or set-based architecture with a biological prediction task.

Single cellsPredicting phenotype from single-cell data

A patient sample holds thousands of cells, but the label (healthy or diseased, responder or not) belongs to the whole sample. CELECTION treats each sample as a bag of cells and uses interpretable multiple-instance learning to predict the phenotype while pointing to the cells that drive it. I led the benchmarking, validation, figure design, and manuscript preparation, including comparisons against eight baseline methods across five sample-level prediction tasks.

Quon Lab, UC Davis. Benchmark code

Gene regulationReading promoters with transformers

ProMod is a self-supervised transformer trained on promoter DNA from more than 100 vertebrate species to predict gene expression. Promoters2Gene (P2G) goes further, combining DNA sequence with chromatin accessibility (ATAC-seq) and histone-modification (ChIP-seq) signals to predict gene expression in specific cell types.

Quon Lab, UC Davis

TokenizationLearning where DNA tokens begin and end

Language models split text into tokens, but DNA has no spaces. GenomeRL is an adaptive DNA tokenization framework in which a reinforcement-learning agent learns token boundaries for promoter detection.

Disease geneticsRare variants and brain disorders

Varformer is a set-based transformer that predicts rare-variant disease risk from regulatory and gene-context features, with a focus on somatic mosaicism in brain disorders.

Quon Lab, UC Davis. Manuscript in preparation

Genome editingWhat makes CRISPR epigenome editing work

EpiCRISPR studies the structural and guide-activity features that determine the outcome of CRISPR epigenome editing, using large-scale guide RNA libraries.

Quon Lab, UC Davis. Manuscript in preparation

Inside transformersBoundary tokens as scaffolding

Large language models lose accuracy abruptly on long, deterministic sequence tasks. AvalancheBreak asks whether periodic boundary or separator tokens delay that drop, and analyzes the behavior with a Sherrington–Kirkpatrick spin-glass lens on Qwen2.5-7B and 14B and Mistral-7B. The answer so far: boundaries act as scaffolding, not as added capability.
